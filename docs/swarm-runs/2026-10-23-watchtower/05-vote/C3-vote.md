# C3 vote: discussion of C1 and C2, and the vote

## Where the three assessments agree

- All three rank schematic-1 first, and all three give the same reason: it is the only one that matches brief 13.3 cell for cell, with the palette and all 29 checks as written.
- All three find the same structure in each file: a 16 x 16 x 16 volume, every block supported, door, ladder, windows, roof steps, and reachability intact.
- All three agree that schematics 2 and 3 share the same 44-cell corbel course at y=9, which brief 5.7 forbids, and that the corbel supports every eave stair.
- All three agree that schematic 2 has the narrower path (x=7 only, 3 cells) and six lanterns, and that schematic 3 has a third hanging lantern at (6,4,9).
- All three agree the one structural point in schematic 1 is the one-block eave overhang. Brief 6.5 allows it, and it is buildable.

## Where I agree or disagree

**C1, on the checks.** I disagree with C1's "Checks" row. C1 lists schematic 2 as "32 listed, with detail" and schematic 3 as "PASS lines only", and does not count failures. Running the brief's own checks (as C2 and I did) shows the brief's check 19, "eave stairs have no support below", fails in schematics 2 and 3. The corbel gives every eave stair a block below. Schematic 2's file handles this by replacing the check with a new check 20 ("every eave stair has a corbel block below") and marking it PASS. Schematic 3's file rewrites check 19 itself, as "every overhanging roof cell rests on the corbel course". Neither file says it has changed the brief's check. That is a substantive omission in C1's comparison. On the brief's own checks, schematic 1 passes 29 of 29, and schematics 2 and 3 each pass 28 of 29.

**C1, on schematic 3's disclosure.** I partly disagree with C1's statement that schematic 3's file "does not say it departs from the brief". Its prose does describe the corbel (the "Route" and "Corbel course" lines, and the eave section say it carries the eave), and it lists three hanging lanterns. What it lacks is any statement that these depart from brief 5.7 or brief 9.3. So "undisclosed as a departure" is right, but "not described" is not. C3's reading is the more accurate one.

**C2, on check counts.** I agree with C2. Schematic 2 scores 28 of 29 and schematic 3 scores 28 of 29 against the brief's own checks, and the failing check is check 19 in both. C2 caught this in the brief-checks row and in its prose; C1 did not.

**C2 and C3, on the render.** I disagree slightly with C2's praise of schematic 3's render "wireframe". I agree with C3 that the outline is an addition beyond the rule's "nothing added". It does not change any block, so it does not affect the vote.

**My own earlier assessment (correction).** My C3 assessment says of schematic 1 that "only two non-air cells lack a block below". That is wrong. The eave at y=10 has 44 cells (40 stairs and 4 corner planks) with air below them, as C1 correctly counts. I counted only 40 stairs, and I left out the planks. The eave is still a permitted cantilever under brief 6.5, so the vote is not affected. The correct statement is that the 44 cells at y=10 are unsupported by design, and only the cells at y=11 and up are required to rest on solid blocks.

**Brief check 19 in schematic 1.** I agree with C2 and C1 that schematic 1 passes check 19 as written, because the brief expects the eave overhang to have no support below. That is the reason schematic 1 is the only one that passes all 29 checks unchanged.

## Vote

Schematic 1 is the most accurate to the brief. It passes every brief check as written and matches brief 13.3 in every cell. It is buildable: every block is supported, and the one cantilevered eave edge is allowed by brief 6.5. Schematics 2 and 3 are buildable too, but they each change the brief's design in ways the brief rules out, and they fail a brief check to get there. The brief decides this, not the render.

If the group wants the cornice, the right step is a deliberate amendment to brief 5.7, with the corbel taken from schematic 3 and the path and lanterns kept from schematic 1. That is a separate decision from this vote.
